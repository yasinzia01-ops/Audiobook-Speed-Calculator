Audible titles are owned permanently with no loan deadline. For speed listening with a budget constraint, Libby wins. For permanent access and richer player features, Audible wins.</p></div>

## What Speed Controls Does Libby Offer?

Libby supports playback speeds from 0.5x to 3x, adjustable in 0.25x increments from within the player interface. The speed control is accessible by tapping the screen while a title is playing, then tapping the speed indicator button. Libby applies pitch correction at all speeds, keeping narration natural-sounding regardless of how fast you play it.

The Libby player also includes a sleep timer, chapter navigation, and bookmarking - all standard features for a full-featured audiobook app. For complete player documentation, see the <a href="https://help.libbyapp.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Libby Help Center</a>.

## What Speed Controls Does Audible Offer?

Audible also supports a 0.5x to 3.5x speed range with pitch correction. Audible's speed interface is generally considered more polished, with a visual dial and finer preset increments including options like 1.1x and 1.2x that Libby does not offer. Audible also includes a Clip and Share feature and more robust chapter markers for navigating long titles at speed.

For detailed speed settings and player features, see <a href="https://www.audible.com/ep/how-to-listen" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Audible's listening guide</a>. Audible requires a paid subscription or per-title purchase to access content.

## Which Is Better When You Are Racing a Loan Deadline?

When a Libby loan is running short, speed becomes critical in a way it simply is not on Audible - because Audible titles never expire. On Libby, going from 1x to 2x speed halves the amount of listening time you need, which can be the difference between finishing and not finishing a 12-hour audiobook in the 3 days you have left.

## Which Platform Should You Choose for Speed Listening?

If cost is a consideration, Libby is the clear choice for speed listeners who want free access to a large audiobook library. The speed controls are fully functional and the pitch correction is excellent. The only trade-off is the loan deadline pressure, which actually motivates many listeners to maintain a consistent daily listening habit.

If you want permanent ownership of titles, richer player features, and finer speed increments, Audible's subscription model is worth considering. Many listeners use both - Libby for popular titles available through their library, and Audible for newer releases or titles with long waitlists.
